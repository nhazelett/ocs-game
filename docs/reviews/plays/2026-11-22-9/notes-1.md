2. Turnover, before day 1: The first page said nothing was on fire. The next said the water point had been down since last week. I wondered what was keeping three hundred people supplied now. The water request later explained that stock on hand should cover day 2.
3. Day 1, Water for Hub / Arrange support: The card quote said a disagreement could leave only half the water delivered, but did not say how likely that was. I could not weigh that risk against writing a contract, so I explored the written order before choosing.
4. Day 1, Water for Hub / written purchase order: The writer buttons said 'done day after you pick two'. I initially read that as the day after I made the choice, rather than a date that would appear once I selected priorities. Scrolling to the Fast/Cheap/Good controls and selecting two resolved it.
5. Day 1, end-of-day plan: I expected End the day to advance the game. It opened a forecast first. That was useful, but 'Still open' repeated that Avery and Novak had nothing to work on, making it look like an unresolved problem even though the same screen said their hours would go to useful walk-in buys.
6. Day 2, Today: Mills told me to join the staff meeting, but no action was visible until I scrolled down through the news. I found the assignment buttons and Join the staff meeting there. I would have looked in Inbox or clicked a person if I had not kept scrolling.
7. Day 3, Desk / Your contracting team: I started the morning with one hour already assigned and did not know why. Clicking You showed that joining the staff meeting had created a standing one-hour commitment. The earlier two-hour Join action had looked like a one-off task to me.
8. Day 3, What happened today: The water card buy delivered half on its very first day, and food service also fell to half because it needed that water. I had seen the water-risk warning, but had not understood that the knock-on problem would also hit another service. I now wanted a way to get written terms without losing the fast water delivery already arranged.
9. Day 4, Island Mart contract page: I looked for a way to settle the disagreement or add written terms. The only action shown was Cancel this card buy. I did not know whether the half delivery was a one-day event or an ongoing dispute that needed action. I left the order running rather than cancel the only water supply; the next day it was full again.

I did not know whether to wait for more misses, find a vendor action somewhere else, or treat the report as information only.
12. Day 16, Debrief: I stopped on the commander's verdict after it said the tutorial was done and all four lines held. Water, forklifts and waste removal each had zero outage days. Mission output was 99% against 95%. I did not get stuck. I left this screen open.

Who was I? A new contracting officer at an already operating island hub for three hundred people, with Avery and Novak in the shop. As a player, I knew contracting generally but none of this game's rules or screens.
What was I trying to do? Keep water, forklifts, waste removal and flying output inside the commander's limits for sixteen days, while learning how this desk turns decisions into actual support.
Did I manage it? Yes. The debrief says all four lines held: zero outage days in the three specified services and 99% mission output. My card water buy still caused one half-supply day and cut food service that day too.
What one change would have helped most? Put the full purchase decision in a compact, readable view so I can see arrangement, supplier, COR, priorities, writer and consequence together. Too much of learning to buy was scrolling a pane until the next required control appeared. I could make the contracting decisions; finding all the pieces of those decisions was the unnecessary work.
