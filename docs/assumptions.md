# Model assumptions and deliberate simplifications

Every place the game departs from how the real mechanism works, with the real rule beside it. The player-facing "model assumptions" panel is generated from the scenario file; this document is the full list for the team. When a simplification is removed, delete its row here and bump the rules patch version.

Legend: **Real** is the mechanism as it exists; **Modeled** is what the sim does; **Why** is the reason for the gap; **Revisit** is the milestone, if any, where it should be reconsidered.

## Thresholds and money

| Topic | Real | Modeled | Why | Revisit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thresholds | FAR 2.101 sets several threshold sets; the contingency, outside-the-U.S. values apply when the acquisition supports a contingency operation and is awarded and performed outside the U.S. | One threshold set per rules file: micro-purchase $40,000, SAT $2,000,000 (effective 1 Oct 2025). Home-station values appear only if a scenario sets them. | Every 1.0 scenario is a deployed, outside-the-U.S. contingency. | When a home-station scenario is written |
| Money | Dollars and cents; obligations, commitments, expenditures are distinct accounting events. | Integer cents. Award obligates; invoices expend; commitments are not modeled. | Determinism and clarity. | None planned |
| Pricing | Quotes, negotiation, price analysis, cost analysis above thresholds. | Vendor quote drawn once per game from a seeded band around a true price; market research narrows the band; the player picks a quote. No negotiation, no typing of prices. | Nick: no dollar entry; pricing arithmetic has a better home on KTHQ. | None planned |
| Color of money | O&M, procurement, MILCON and others, each with rules on use and period; appropriations may be used only for their purpose (31 U.S.C. 1301(a)), and a funding document is certified for a stated requirement. | O&M only. Lines have an amount, a release day, a fiscal-year end and an optional purpose: `capabilities`, the requirements the line is certified for. Paying another capability from it is a hard stop in the editor, for awards, IDIQ guarantees and definitizations; ratifications pick only a line whose purpose fits (M3-03). | Scope control for 1.0: purpose by capability is the wrong-type stop SPEC 3 asks for without appropriation law. Labeled as a large simplification in the spec. | Post-1.0 |
| Funds certification | FM certifies availability before obligation. | A certification lag in days (scenario parameter); the action waits. | Enough to teach "funding in hand before award." | None planned |
| Fiscal year end | Annual appropriations expire for new obligations at the end of the fiscal year; the next year's money arrives when it is apportioned and released, often late (a continuing resolution or a slow release). A severable service may cross the year end if its period is at most 12 months (10 U.S.C. 3133). A service whose period begins in the next year is that year's need (bona fide need, 31 U.S.C. 1502(a)); it can be awarded subject to availability of funds (FAR 32.703-2) and starts only when the money comes. | A line's `expiresDay` is its fiscal-year end and `availableDay` its planned release. Event effect `funding.late` (seeded `daysRange`) pushes a line's release back, with a comptroller notice the day it fires. The comptroller also warns 10 days before a year end with money unobligated, records the expiry, a day with no usable money, and each release. An award, definitization or ratification that completes after the line's year end finds no money (rejected or left waiting). A service longer than 365 days that runs past its line's year end is a hard stop. Bona fide need is not modeled: a service may be paid from a line whose year ends before the service starts; subject-to-availability awards are not modeled. | The trap is the gap, not the appropriations law; the 12-month rule is one line of code and keeps 400-day awards honest. | M3-07 if its fiscal-year turn needs subject-to-availability awards |

## Instruments

| Topic | Real | Modeled | Why | Revisit |

| SF 44 | Purchase order-invoice-voucher for on-the-spot, over-the-counter purchases, with conditions on use and dollar limits by situation. | Same-day cash buy capped at the micro-purchase threshold; draws cash on hand with a replenishment lag; needs a paying agent. Recurring services on repeated SF 44s roll an execution-confusion curve and are tagged in the debrief. | Captures the consequence Nick cares about (no terms, cash runs out) without the form. | None planned |
| GPC | Cardholder and billing-official structure, single-purchase and monthly limits, merchant acceptance, convenience checks, expanded-use procedures. | Available or not (by prep choice or a 10 to 20 day work item); per-vendor acceptance and surcharge; single and monthly limits. | Purchase order | SF 1449 or equivalent; synopsis and posting rules with contingency exceptions; evaluation; award. | 1 to 2 shop-days with a system, times a manual factor without; written terms; invoice path with a manual error curve. No synopsis wait (contingency flexibilities assumed to apply). No evaluation play. | Evaluation and source selection are a different game. | None planned |
| BPA | An agreement, not a contract; calls are the obligations; vendors may decline calls; multiple BPAs are common. | Vehicle with scope tags, call limit and ordering period; calls can be declined (base probability scaled by market tempo and vendor reliability); the vendor can withdraw with notice. | IDIQ | FAR 16.504: a minimum that is more than nominal and a maximum (ceiling), an ordering period, scope; the minimum is obligated at award and owed if not ordered; fair opportunity among multiple awardees (FAR 16.505(b)); IDIQs above the SAT follow full procedures. | Single award only (M3-06). 8 to 15 shop-days to establish; the minimum guarantee is at least 5% of the ceiling (the "more than nominal" floor), obligated from a funding line at award; orders spend it first, then new money; it counts as obligated whether ordered or not and a terminated order never returns it. Binding: orders are never declined. Hard stops on scope, ceiling, ordering period, place of performance; the ceiling is capped by the warrant. No extra time for a ceiling above the SAT. Vendor capacity is still finite: orders are accepted above it and deliver short (capacity event, oldest order served first after the vendor's other work). | UCA | DFARS 217.74: HCA approval; NTE; obligations capped at 50% of NTE before definitization, 75% after a qualifying proposal; definitize by the earlier of 180 days after issuance or 50% obligated; profit and cost-risk considerations at definitization. | All of the first sentence except the last clause: no profit or cost-risk adjustment. Approval is through the SCO channel with a lag. The qualifying proposal is a seeded vendor event. Definitization is a work item that needs funding attached to the definitized amount; it never releases funds by itself. | The caps and the clock are the lesson; profit math is not. | None planned |
| Inherited arrangements (M3-16) | A relief-in-place hands over running contracts with their files: periods of performance, obligations to date on specific lines, modifications, UCA approvals and definitization schedules, invoices in work. | An inherited row may set `quantity`, `dailyCostCents`, `obligatedCents` (for performance from day 1, already counted against `fundingLine`) and, for a UCA, `ucaState` (NTE, obligations before day 1, issue day, proposal day, definitized, optional `quotedTotalCents`). Earlier obligations count toward the NTE caps, the 50% trigger and the definitized price but not toward this game's cost; the 180-day clock runs from the issue day, so a UCA issued long enough ago is overdue on day 1. Mobilization before day 1 is not charged. Costs incurred before day 1, modifications and invoices in work are not carried. Rows that set none of this behave as before (vendor capacity, vendor price, no obligation). | Lets a sustainment scenario start mid-operation with real clocks without modeling the last rotation's books. | M3-17 (hidden problems), M3-19 (notice and claims) |
| Hidden inherited problems and the file review (M3-17) | A relief-in-place handover is a briefing and a stack of contract files. Problems the outgoing shop did not flag (a UCA past due for definitization, an IDIQ near its ceiling, a vendor whose performance was worse than reported) are in the files for whoever reads them, or surface when higher HQ, the ceiling or a failure finds them. | An inherited row with `visibleProblem: false` keeps its problem out of the views (`SessionView.ProblemHidden`): a UCA shows as an ordinary contract (no clocks, no overdue or proposal notice), an IDIQ order hides the vehicle's ceiling and ordered value, and `reportedReliability` is shown in place of the vendor's true record. The Sim runs on the true state throughout. A `reviewFiles` action costs rules `fileReview.hoursPerArrangement` shop hours per file (placeholder 4, half a person-day) and, when worked, writes a Knowledge record per file with what it shows. A higher-HQ inquiry about a past-due UCA brings that one to light without a review. Host nation support has no file of the shop's and is not reviewable. An order against a hidden IDIQ past its ceiling is still refused, and the refusal names the ceiling: that is where an unreviewed shop finds out. | One investment (a day of shop time in week one) against several late, expensive discoveries, which is the sustainment trap the M3-07 scenario is built on. | M3-07 (Sustainment), M3-21 (validate and game) |
| ACSA | Reimbursable logistic support, supplies and services with partner nations under agreement (10 U.S.C. 2341-2350); ordering, payment-in-kind and replacement-in-kind options, and reimbursement procedures. | M3-06: an order (`acsaOrder`) only when `actors.acsaPartner.present`; 1 to 2 shop days after FM certification, then 2 to 5 days to start. Partner stock is a daily ceiling per capability shared by orders on the same days, not a depleting pool. The partner supplies and runs the capability's core line (operator and maintenance included), never supply lines to other capabilities. Cash reimbursement only, at a scenario price, obligated in full when the order is placed (the ledger line); unperformed days return on withdrawal. No cure notice, COR or payment errors. Withdrawn by the scenario event `acsa.withdraw` (a capability or all, with notice), after which new orders for it stop. Confirming partner agreements in prep still has no mechanical effect. | Scope; keeps the lesson (cheap, sometimes the only source, and not yours to keep). | Payment and replacement in kind, stock depletion, M3-07 |
| Host nation support | Agreements vary widely: bilateral or multilateral HNS agreements and implementing arrangements set what the host nation provides, where, and who pays. | M3-06: a request (`hostNationRequest`) only when `actors.hostNation.agreementExists`; 1 to 2 shop days, then 3 to 10 days to start (slow to establish). The agreement lists what it covers: a daily capacity per capability, shared by requests on the same days, and optionally the sites it covers. The host nation supplies and runs the capability's core line (operator and maintenance included), never supply lines to other capabilities. The shop's cost share is a scenario price per unit-day, often zero (no funding line, no FM certification lag); when set, obligated in full when the request is placed, and unperformed days return on withdrawal. Reliability per scenario (default 0.9). No cure notice, COR or payment errors; no negotiation model. Withdrawn by the scenario event `hostNation.withdraw` (a capability or all; notice from the event, else rules `withdrawalNoticeDays`), which ends inherited host-nation arrangements as well as requested ones and stops new requests. Confirming partner agreements in prep still has no mechanical effect. | Scope; keeps the lesson (cheap and slow to start, and it can go away, including the support the force arrived with). | Agreement negotiation, M3-07 |
| Stateside or off-island sourcing | Goods bought from stateside or regional suppliers move by the strategic lift system (airlift by priority, sealift) to a port of entry, clear customs, then move forward; higher-priority cargo displaces lower. | M3-06: an off-island vendor is a vendor with `lift` modes; its island is the port of entry, and onward delivery uses the surface links like any vendor. The award names `lift` (airlift or sealift); SF 44 is refused; consumables go by sealift only (they do not fly in v0). Lead time adds the airlift queue (rules `lift.airliftQueueDays`, 7 to 14) or sealift transit (30 to 45). On the due day an airlift shipment can be bumped (rules chance, up to `airliftMaxBumps` times, 3 to 7 days each), then one customs roll can hold it (2 to 6 days); each delay moves the whole period of performance later, paid duration unchanged. Lift priority is not a player choice, and there is no lift cost or pipeline capacity: once arrived, supply flows daily as for any vendor. Chances are placeholders tuned in playtest. | Scope; keeps the lesson (cheaper and larger, delayed by design, payoff in month two, and a bridge is needed). | Lift priority requests, a resupply pipeline for consumables, M3-07 |
| External support contract | Program-specific request and approval chain, task order competition, pricing, mobilization; the requiring activity sends funds to the external contracting office; local administration through a COR. | Requested from the OCS seat (M3-05): the shop prepares the request (2 to 3 shop days, after FM certification), then the SCO and higher approve it in 10 to 20 days, always (no refusal or conditions on the merits yet); the external office awards and obligates the whole task order from the unit's funding line on the approval day, or the request lapses if that line can no longer pay; mobilization 14 to 30 days. No competition; price, capacity and reliability are scenario parameters (`actors.externalSupport`); minimum useful duration 60 days; minimum useful scope is the core, operator and maintenance lines (self-managed). The contractor brings its own consumables, so it is supplied on the site's island and does not ride the surface links. No warrant or threshold limit applies (another office's authority). A COR may be named; performance issues and cure notices work as for local contracts. A request cannot be withdrawn before award. No program is named in content. | Keeps content from going stale and keeps the lesson (start it early so the bridge can end). | Withdrawal before award, SCO conditions, task order modifications, M3-07 |
| Transition plan | Transitions between arrangements (bridge to enduring, contract to contract) are planned by the requiring activity and the contracting office together: overlapping periods of performance, a handoff with acceptance by the unit, phase-in and phase-out obligations in the contracts, and early ends by modification or partial termination for convenience (FAR 49). | M3-05: `planTransition` names an outgoing arrangement and an incoming arrangement or external support request at the same site and capability. Handoff `incomingDelivering` (default): after the incoming delivers at full on `overlapDays` consecutive days (rules `transition.defaultOverlapDays`, 2), the plan starts an ordinary termination of the outgoing, so its last day is the next day if the shop has the hours. Handoff `onDay`: the outgoing's last day is the cut-over day. The plan resolves the day after the outgoing's last day: clean if the incoming is running then, a gap otherwise. The planner projects the incoming's start (an external request's range from preparation, approval and mobilization, ignoring other queued work) and flags a projected gap on the morning board once the outgoing ends within rules `transition.warnDays` (5). Planning costs no shop hours; there is no phase-in or phase-out scope, no handoff acceptance step, no extension of the outgoing (the player bridges with another award), and an early end of a UCA returns no money (as for any UCA termination). Owner (`cco` or `ocs`) is a label on the plan and in the debrief, with no mechanical effect. | Keeps the lesson (the gap is visible before it is real, and an enduring solution has to be started early enough to overlap) without contract-modification mechanics. | Extension by modification, M3-07 sustainment scenario |
| Coordination | Informal arrangements between units; no contract. | A zero-cost arrangement that can be withdrawn when scenario events say the other party's priorities changed. | Teaches the difference between coordination and contractual execution. | None planned |
| Unauthorized commitments | FAR 1.602-3: ratification by an official with authority, with approval levels, legal review and a finding that the price is fair; the government may decline to ratify. | Situation 1 (handshake) and situation 2 (out-of-scope work under a contract) arrive as inbox decisions (SPEC 4.5). Ratify costs `ratificationShopDays` and obligates what was delivered plus a 7-day bridge; no approval levels, legal review or price finding. | Teaches the decision and its consequences without the paperwork chain. | M3 (approval levels by amount) |
| Handshake vendor patience | A vendor delivering unpaid on a unit's word eventually stops; how long varies. | Seeded 5 to 15 days from the day it is opened while it stays open or parked; ratifying stops the clock. On walk-off the vendor declines this office's work (50%) or quotes it 25% higher (50%) for the rest of the game. | One number the debrief can explain. | Playtest |
| After ratification | A ratified commitment covers past performance; continuing need goes on a contract. | The vendor keeps delivering on the unit's word, with no terms; deliveries past the bridge are owed and show as incurred but not obligated until the player contracts. | Shows the liability without ending supply the player never replaced. | None planned |
| Parked or ignored commitments | Long-unratified commitments and vendor claims draw scrutiny from higher headquarters. | After 10 days unresolved, an HHQ inquiry costing 24 shop hours: for a handshake, why it is unratified; for out-of-scope work, the vendor's claim. | Makes parking a cost later instead of a free pass. | Playtest |
| Refusing a handshake | Stopping the vendor is lawful; the need remains and the commander notices. | Supply stops the same day; a commander-confidence hit is counted. The priority-order penalty in SPEC 4.5 lands with commander priority allocation, which the sim does not have yet. | Priority allocation is not modeled in M1. | M2 |
| Precedent | Units that get away with going around contracting do it again. | Each site's unit has a habit level (0 to 60%): parked or refused handshakes and handshakes open 5+ days raise it; ratifying within 3 days lowers it; ratifying out-of-scope work raises it a little. With a habit above zero, a unit rolls once a day to make its own handshake when a capability was down yesterday and nothing on contract supplies it, never with a vendor that walked off. | A visible, explainable cause for the second and third handshake. | Playtest |
| Terminations | FAR Part 49; cure and show-cause notices for default; settlement for convenience; legal review; deobligation of the unperformed portion. | A termination is a work item. The unperformed portion of a fully obligated award is deobligated: back to its line while the line is current, lapsed if it has expired. SCO guidance and dispute probabilities arrive with the SCO channel. No settlement cost yet, except on a contract with a notice clause (next row). | The money lesson without settlement accounting. | M3 for settlement cost and SCO guidance |
| Termination notice and vendor claims (M3-19) | FAR Part 49: a termination for convenience entitles the contractor to a settlement for the work cut short and its costs of stopping; many service contracts also require written notice before the government ends performance early. Skipping it invites a claim that someone has to negotiate. A contractor terminated for default has no such claim. | Only contracts with a notice clause (an inherited row's `noticeDays`) carry it. A `terminate` with `withNotice` ends performance `noticeDays` after the termination work is done and costs nothing more. Without notice performance ends at once and the vendor files a claim: rules `claims.settlementDays` (placeholder 10) days of its daily cost, capped at the days cut short, settled as `claims.settleShopHours` (placeholder 16) of shop work and charged to the arrangement's line when settled. No claim when the vendor is in default after a cure notice (M2-18). A transition plan's cut-over terminates without notice, so a cut-over on such a contract draws a claim unless the player gave notice first. Award terminations are unchanged: awards carry no notice clause yet. | Makes "terminate now" against "terminate with notice" a real trade (days of overlap against money and shop hours), which the M3-07 bFuel chain needs. | Notice clauses on awards; negotiated settlements |
| Warrants | Warrant limits and authority. | A scenario parameter; null means sufficient. A prep choice can raise it. | Not a mechanic until someone asks. | On request |

## Debrief tags (M2-08)

SPEC 4: avoidable means the information existed; discoverable means it was available for a cost; unforeseeable means no path to know. A gap takes the most controllable tag among its factors.

| Factor | Tag | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing supply line | Avoidable | An arrangement for a capability that brings this one (power's fuel supply) was bought without that line; the requirement's dependency checklist showed it from the day the card was submitted. |
| Uncovered requirement | Avoidable | A submitted requirement had nothing supplying it on a day it was needed; the debrief names the last arrangement that ended and the next one that started late. |
| Coordination withdrawn | Avoidable | A coordination was the only source and was withdrawn; a coordination is not a contract. Counted only if no contract supplied the need after the withdrawal. |
| ACSA withdrawn (M3-06) | Avoidable | An ACSA order was the only source and the partner withdrew it; nothing contractual was behind it. Counted only if no contract supplied the need after the withdrawal. |
| Host nation support withdrawn (M3-06) | Avoidable when requested, Discoverable when inherited | The host nation withdrew support that was the only source and nothing contractual was behind it. Support the player requested is the player's choice; inherited support was host-nation support from day 1, so its withdrawability was there to be found. Counted only if no contract supplied the need after the withdrawal. |
| Lift delay (M3-06) | Unforeseeable | A gap day after an off-island delivery was due and before it arrived, because the airlift bumped it or customs held it. Waiting for the planned arrival is not this factor; that shows as an uncovered requirement. |
| Transition gap (M3-05) | Avoidable when flagged, Unforeseeable otherwise | A gap day after a transition plan's outgoing arrangement ended and before its incoming one was running, with nothing else supplying the need. Avoidable when the planner flagged the gap before the outgoing's last day; unforeseeable when it did not (the incoming was lost or slipped on the last day). |
| Owner not engaged (M2R-06) | Avoidable | A gap day on which nothing at all supplied a capability that has a usual owner other than the office (rules `usualOwner`; fuel: the J4 through the ACSA partner) while the partner here had stock to spare. The owner was there to ask from day 1 and the checklist named them; a stopgap that was bought and then failed has its own factor instead. Text says whether the J4 was never asked, asked late, or asked and refused. |
| Handshake lost | Avoidable | A handshake was parked or left open until the vendor walked off, or refused with nothing in its place. |
| Off-island consumable | Discoverable, or Avoidable once surveyed | Deliveries from another island wait on the surface link; before a survey of the site's island returned, the dependency was discoverable; after, it was known. Weather closures in the three days before are named as context. |
| Demand above supply | Avoidable | Supply sized below demand: continuous capacity below the load, or a consumable rate (a day) below daily use; population steps are in the plan from day 1. |
| Vendor failure | Avoidable without operator or maintenance lines, otherwise Unforeseeable; an inherited vendor whose record the handover overstated: Discoverable until its file is reviewed, Avoidable after (M3-17); after an authored drop that a performance issue opened before it had warned of: Avoidable (M3-18) | Missing lines raise daily failure odds. | UCA stop work | Avoidable; a hidden inherited UCA: Discoverable while its clock was unfound on the stop day (M3-17) | Incurred costs passed the obligation cap or the money on the line; the overdue date is named when there is one. | Execution confusion | Avoidable | A recurring service on a buy with no written terms. |
| BPA call declined | Unforeseeable | A BPA is not binding; any call can be declined. |
| Priority deferred | Avoidable | An award that would have supplied the capability (directly or through a supply line) was held back behind other sites' shop work because that site's commander lost confidence (a refused handshake), and it had not started on the gap day. |
| Population jump (M2-19) | Unforeseeable for 7 days after the arrival, Avoidable after | An unplanned arrival (scenario `site.populationJump`) put demand above arrangements that covered the plan's numbers. Arrangements below even the planned demand keep the Demand above supply factor. |
