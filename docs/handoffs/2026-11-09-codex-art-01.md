**Field atlas:** a quiet printed strategy board in ivory, sage, ochre, pine ink and burnt sienna. Bitter slab-serif headings and Barlow body accompany a fictional cut-paper/ink portrait and outlined equipment on hatched coasts. Crosses plus the actual 0% caption identify outages without relying on color. The lowest carry-through cost: one shared paper/ink theme, a vector icon set and a matching cast portrait pass; most current 2D layouts remain useful. Its tradeoff is less visual depth than the 3D direction.

**Night watch:** a dark signal-room treatment in navy, sea-glass teal and warm amber, with Barlow Regular/SemiBold. People use a duotone identity illustration, and sites use luminous outlines, contour rings and explicit outage crosses/labels. Medium carry-through cost: one contrast-tested dark theme, matching portraits/icons and restrained map-material effects, reusing most desk layouts. Its risk is feeling like an operations console, so the person's message stays prominent and the screen avoids a dense diagnostic HUD.

**Tabletop:** a tangible miniature world in bone, moss, ochre, dusty blue and terracotta, with Atkinson Hyperlegible Regular/Bold and a faceted fictional portrait study. The map contains actual Godot 3D generators, tanks, tents and huts, an orthographic camera and directional lighting; dark equipment and explicit power/support captions come from the same tile states. The highest carry-through cost: coherent 3D models/materials/lighting, camera and label work, plus a matching 2D theme on every desk/opening/debrief screen. It provides the clearest path to tangible deliveries later, without inventing movement now.
