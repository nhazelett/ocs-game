# Scenario skeletons (prose)

Approved by Nick, 7 Nov 2026. These are the five 1.0 scenarios in the form the author writes them; Fable turns each into `scenario.json` once the schema (M0-04) lands. Numbers here are first-pass and become tunables. Order of build: Spoke B (first playable), tutorial, sustainment, surge, drawdown.

Each skeleton covers six things: the situation on day 1, what is already in place, what is hidden until someone looks, the seeded event that bites, the trap a first-time player walks into, and what good looks like at the end.

## 1. Spoke B power (first playable)

Hub island open with 600 personnel. Spoke B, an outer island, opens day 7 with 180 personnel growing to 260 by day 20; the growth is in the plan but easy to miss. Requirement 0007 from the advance team: six 60 kW generators, Spoke B, required day 7, 90 days; its fuel, operator and maintenance lines are off. In place: one base operating support BPA (Vendor C) covering equipment rental, scope excludes fuel delivery. Market: Vendor A (generators only, 4-day lead), Vendor B (turnkey power with fueling and a technician, 9-day lead), both at estimated confidence. One O&M funding line, $900,000, expires day 75. Shop: two people. Hidden until surveyed: no grid, no fuel point, reached only by a twice-weekly barge from the hub and a light aircraft; hub-island vendors depend on the barge, the one spoke-island vendor does not. Seeded: weather stops the barge days 16 to 18; Vendor B's qualifying proposal lands about day 40 if a UCA is used; the hub fuels flight withdraws any fuel-run coordination once it has run more than four days. Trap: buying what the requirement says and nothing more. Good: power continuous at Spoke B, the enduring arrangement started early enough that the bridge can end, the UCA (if any) definitized before the funding line expires, growth at day 20 covered.

## 2. Tutorial: hub, week one

Hub island only, 20 days, 300 personnel, already open with the basics running. In place: billeting and dining on an existing BPA. Three requirements arrive on days 1, 4 and 9: bottled water for a heat advisory, a forklift rental because the island's one MHE broke, and a waste contract because the honey trucks borrowed from the host nation are being pulled back. Each teaches one instrument: water is a GPC or cash buy (lesson: cash on hand and vendor acceptance); the forklift is a BPA call that the vendor declines (lesson: non-binding means non-binding); waste is a written purchase order (lesson: a service needs terms and a COR). Hidden: the directory is accurate except one vendor's capacity. Seeded: the manual payment path drops an invoice on day 12 and the shop loses a day. Trap: paying for the waste service on SF 44s because it was faster. Good: all three covered by day 15, no unauthorized commitments, one survey ordered.

## 3. Sustainment: everything is already running

Hub plus three spokes; day 1 of play is day 90 of the operation; 60 days. The player inherits eleven arrangements from the last rotation: two UCAs (one with 40 days left to definitize, one past due that nobody noticed), a BPA whose ordering period ends day 20, an IDIQ at 85% of ceiling, a COR who rotates home day 10, and a fuel vendor whose reliability has been slipping in the reports. Nothing is on fire on day 1; everything is on a clock. Hidden: the past-due UCA, the IDIQ ceiling, and the true fuel reliability (the outgoing COR's reports were generous). Seeded: the fuel vendor defaults on day 25 unless re-sourced or cured; a funding line expires at fiscal year end on day 45. Trap: spending the first two weeks on new unit requests while the inherited clocks run out. Good: every transition done with overlap, the past-due UCA definitized before higher HQ asks, the fuel vendor replaced or cured before the default, the termination of the slipping vendor run through the SCO so the dispute event never fires.

## 4. Surge: company arrives

Hub plus two spokes, 45 days, running normally on day 1. Day 8: spoke population doubles on a week's notice. Day 15: a sister-service element lands on the same spoke island with its own contracting shop. The first week is capacity: demand exceeds every arrangement's effective supply, buffers drain, and the player chooses what to let fail (endurance first, sortie generation last). Then the joint turn: both shops hit the same four island vendors, quotes climb, duplicate fuel and waste arrangements appear, and the other service's units start directing your vendors. Hidden: the other service already holds a theater vehicle that could cover water for both. Seeded: price-war event day 18; an unauthorized commitment from the other service's unit day 20; the SCO offers boards unprompted if responsiveness is high, otherwise waits to be asked. Trap: treating the arrival as a contracting problem instead of an OCS problem and never opening the OCS seat. Good: JRRB and JCSB stood up by day 22, duplicates merged, the shared vehicle found, the spoke never without power or water for more than a day.

## 5. Drawdown: close Spoke C without stranding anything

Hub plus three spokes, 45 days. Order on day 1: Spoke C closes day 35; population steps down 40% on day 10, 70% on day 25, zero on day 35; its 200 people move to Spoke A, which was sized for 150. Every Spoke C arrangement ends differently: the generator rental has a 30-day notice clause; the waste contract runs to day 60, so the player decides whether to terminate for convenience or ride it out; the billeting BPA calls just stop; the fuel IDIQ serves both C and A, so it transitions rather than ends. Hidden: Spoke A's water capacity is already at its limit. Seeded: the last barge off Spoke C is day 33, after which anything still there stays; a vendor claims for early termination if notice was not given. Trap: closing arrangements in the order they are listed instead of the order the drawdown needs (power is needed to pack out; Spoke A's water line has to grow before day 10, not day 35). Good: nothing obligated past day 35 at Spoke C, no claim, Spoke A absorbing 200 people without a gap, the hub shop's hours not eaten by paperwork during the move.
